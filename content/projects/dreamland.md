## Objective

Human fetal development is still largely described from sections: tissue cut, stained and read
slice by slice, with the three-dimensional architecture reconstructed in the reader's head.
DREAMLAND images whole fetal organs in three dimensions without cutting them, and does it with two
modalities that see different things — synchrotron phase-contrast tomography for cellular-scale
architecture, MRI for anatomy and tissue contrast over the whole organ.

A first imaging campaign covered about a dozen individuals and some 180 organs; new samples
arrive at a steady rate through CHU Grenoble Alpes. At that volume, the bottleneck is no longer
the acquisition but the analysis.

## Approach

Samples are imaged by synchrotron phase-contrast tomography and by MRI, and the two modalities are registered so that the microscopic architecture can be read against the macroscopic anatomy. A postdoctoral position, co-funded between 3T at IAB and the GIN, develops the deep-learning segmentation pipeline; it is written to be reused across the whole consortium rather than for one study.
