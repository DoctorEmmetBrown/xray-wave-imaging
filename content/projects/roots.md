## Objective

Biological tissue is full of oriented structure that no detector can resolve directly: collagen
fibres, dentinal tubules, myocardial fibres, the alveolar walls of the lung. The directional
dark-field signal carries that orientation — every pixel scatters the beam more in one direction
than another — and modulation-based imaging can record it in a single exposure, on a synchrotron
or on a laboratory source.

ROOTS asks how much of that information can actually be recovered, and how reliably. The field
currently describes the anisotropy with a rank-2 tensor, three numbers per pixel; ROOTS builds the
retrieval that goes beyond it, quantifies its stability, and validates it against direct
small-angle scattering on the same samples.
