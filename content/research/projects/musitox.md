## Objective

Most interesting processes happen at more than one scale at a time. A battery electrode
degrades atom by atom and fails millimetre by millimetre; a snowpack metamorphoses grain by
grain and avalanches by the slope. Tomography usually forces a choice between the two: a wide
field at coarse resolution, or a fine field that misses the context.

MUSITOX develops methods for imaging **both scales simultaneously**, in three dimensions and
in real time. That means extending X-ray microtomography in three directions at once: towards
time-resolved acquisition, towards phase contrast — so that weakly absorbing phases stop being
invisible — and towards unconventional acquisition geometries that a classical reconstruction
would not accept. Machine learning enters on the acquisition and reconstruction side, where it
buys back the signal that shorter exposures give away.

## Why it is cross-disciplinary

The programme is deliberately built across communities that do not usually share an instrument:

- **materials** — composites, metals, batteries;
- **public health** — bone architecture and its disorders, osteoarthritis in particular;
- **environment and engineering** — flow in porous media, suspension mechanics, snow.

Around fifty researchers from nine Grenoble laboratories take part, with the CMTC and GRICAD
platforms, spanning the PEM, PAGE and CBS research clusters. For our group it is the project
that keeps the methods honest: an algorithm tuned on soft tissue meets a metal foam and a wet
granular medium, and its hidden assumptions surface quickly.
