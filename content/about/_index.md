## The group

The X-ray Wave Imaging Group develops wave-optical X-ray imaging methods — phase contrast,
dark-field, single-pixel sensing for fluorescence and scattering, and the inverse problems needed to
turn any of them into a measurement. The **MIAI FANFARES chair**, held with CREATIS and CEA IRIG,
anchors that computational side of the work. Physicists, applied
mathematicians, image-processing specialists, biologists and medical doctors work side by side here,
which is the only way a method ever makes it from an optical bench to a question someone actually
asked. We are part of the **Translational Theranostics team (3T)** at the Institute for Advanced
Biosciences in Grenoble (Inserm U1209 / CNRS UMR 5309 / Université Grenoble Alpes).

The relationship is deliberate. Methods developed here are meant to answer biomedical questions
posed inside 3T and at CHU Grenoble Alpes, not to remain instrumentation for its own sake.

## Instruments

**Laboratory.** A Xenocs Xeuss 3.0 operating at 8.6 keV, λ = 1.44 Å, with a 75 µm pixel pitch and a
variable sample-to-detector distance. It is a SAXS instrument, which means dark-field measurements
can be validated against direct scattering data on the same sample without moving it.

**Synchrotron.** We run regular campaigns at ESRF, ALBA and SOLEIL, on beamlines suited to
propagation phase contrast and to monochromatic dark-field over a wide energy range.

## Can you measure my sample?

Often, yes — and the honest answer usually depends on three things: how thick the sample is, whether
the structure of interest is above or below the pixel, and whether it can travel. Write to us with a
photograph and a sentence about what you are trying to see.
